Noun Incorporation (NI)

When a noun (argument attached to the verb) gets incorporated into the verb (mostly becomes a single word).

Example

In Yucatec Mayan (spoken in Mexico and Belize; SVO)

Before incorporation, verb and D.O. are separate words:

tin’akahče’
t-in-č’ak-Ø’ahče’
COMP-1SG-chop-it-ASPtree

'I chopped a tree'

Noun incorporated into a single word:

č’akče’nahen
č’ak-če’-n-ah-en
chop-tree-ANTIP-ASP-1SG.ABS

'I chopped wood' (can cause slight change in meaning)

Classified into 3 types (effect on syntax):

  1. Classified NI

    Process of incorporation does not affect the transitivity of the verb and it can take an object.

    Example

    In Oneida (Iroquoian; VSO)

    Before incorporation (independent NP pig):

    wa?-k-hnihn-u:neka?i:knkóskos
    FACT-1SG.AG-buy-ASPDETDEMpig

    'I bought this pig'

    After incorporation (object serves as the type of incorporated animal and cannot be replaced with something that is not an animal):

    wa?-k-aeskwa-hnihn-u:neka?i:knkóskos
    FACT-1SG.AG-animal-buy-ASPDETDEMpig

    'I bought this pig'

  2. Compound NI

    Process of incorporation affects the transitivity of the verb (becomes intransitive) and it cannot take an argument or object.

    Example

    In Pohnpeian (Austronesian; SVO)

    Before incorporation (separate verb and object with transitive marker on verb):

    Ipahnihkos-elikonehn
    Iwillpleat-TRdressa

    'I will pleat a dress'

    After incorporation (becomes a single word and the argument is lost):

    Ipahnihkos-Ø-likon
    Iwillpleat-INTR-dress

    'I will dress-pleat' or 'I will pleat a dress'

    In English (SVO)

    Before incorporation (transitive verb):

    Samwatch-esbird-s
    Samwatch-3SG.PRSbird-PL

    'Sam watches birds'

    After incorporation (becomes intransitive and -s is lost):

    Sambird-watch-es
    Sambird-watch-3SG.PRS

    'Sam goes bird-watching'

  3. Pseudo NI (Massam)

    Process of incorporation is at phrasal level (verb and object don’t merge morphologically). It reduces transitivity (based on case markings).

    Easiestto see in VSO languages, where subject intervenes between verb and object.

    Example

    In Niuean (Austronesian; VSO)

    Before incorporation:

    koefanogonogoalautolukehetanlologo
    PRSlistenABStheytoPLsong

    'They were listening to songs'

    After incorporation (subject is shifted to the end):

    koefanogonogolologoalautolu
    PRSlistensongABSthey

    'They were listening to songs'

    In Hindi (Indo-Aryan, SOV)

    Before incorporation (specific object marked with ko):

    Anughorekobechegi
    Anuhorse.OBLACCsell.FUT.3SG.F

    'Anu will sell the horse.'

    After incorporation (-ko case marker lost and bare object):

    Anughorabechegi
    Anuhorsesell.FUT.3SG.F

    'Anu will horse-sell.' i.e. 'Anu will sell a horse/horses'

    Massam’s claim - Objects don’t incorporate morphologically into verb but are adjacent (visible in modified nouns). Eg. In Niuean:

    Incorporated sentence:

    NeinukofekonoaMele
    PSTdrinkcoffeebitterABSMele

    'Mary drank bitter coffee'

    Here, the adjective kono comes along with the noun kofe, and hence the verb is joined to the whole phrase (NP), not just one word.
